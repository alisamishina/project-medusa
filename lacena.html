La Cena in Emmaus

I chose the painting called Supper at Emmaus. First, I try to find it in the ARCO ontology using only the name:

Query 1

PREFIX rdfs: http://www.w3.org/2000/01/rdf-schema# PREFIX arco: https://w3id.org/arco/ontology/arco/ SELECT DISTINCT ?painting ?label WHERE { ?painting a arco:HistoricOrArtisticProperty ; rdfs:label ?label . FILTER(REGEX(?label, "la cena in emmaus", "i")) } LIMIT 100

Result 1

If we write only the name of the painting, there are too many results, because there are multiple different works with the same name. So I narrow it down by adding the author, Caravaggio, and language of the page - English

Query 2

PREFIX rdfs: http://www.w3.org/2000/01/rdf-schema# PREFIX a-cd: https://w3id.org/arco/ontology/context-description/ SELECT DISTINCT ?painting ?label ?authorName WHERE { ?painting rdfs:label ?label ; a-cd:hasAuthor ?author . ?author rdfs:label ?authorName . FILTER(regex(?label, "la cena in emmaus", "i")) FILTER(regex(?authorName, "caravaggio", "i")) FILTER(lang(?label) = "en") } LIMIT 10

Result 2

The result of the query is pretty good, it gives only three links. One of them has the author name just Caravaggio, while the other two have Merisi Michelangelo Detto Caravaggio

Creating Prompts to Retrieve Information

Then I asked to provide some information about the painting. I used Zero-shot promt:
Comparing Caravaggio's two versions of the *Supper at Emmaus* reveals a clear evolution in his style, shifting from grand theatricality to quiet introspection. ### 1. The Mood and Lighting * **1601 Version (London):** Highly theatrical and dramatic. The scene is illuminated by a stark, sharp spotlight effect that makes the figures and objects pop vividly against a dark background. * **1606 Version (Milan):** Much darker, softer, and more somber. The light feels faded or extinguished, creating a hushed, melancholic, and deeply contemplative atmosphere. ### 2. Depiction of Jesus Christ * **1601 Version (London):** Christ is portrayed as clean-shaven (beardless) with softer, slightly more idealized or youthful features, wearing bright red and white garments. His arms are thrown wide open in a dramatic, sweeping gesture of revelation. * **1606 Version (Milan):** Christ has a short beard and appears older, thinner, and more weathered. He wears more subdued blue-green clothing, and his gesture of blessing is restrained and quiet rather than expansive. ### 3. The Table and Still Life * **1601 Version (London):** Features a lavish, abundant spread, including a large basket of fruit that teeters precariously over the edge of the table (showcasing Caravaggio's mastery of trompe-l'œil). * **1606 Version (Milan):** The meal is sparse and stripped down, consisting only of simple bread, a modest jug of wine, and a few greens. ### 4. Cast of Characters * **1601 Version (London):** Features the two disciples, the innkeeper, and Jesus—all male. The disciples' physical reactions are explosive, with one nearly leaping out of his chair and thrusting his elbows and hands toward the viewer. * **1606 Version (Milan):** Introduces an elderly serving woman holding a dish, adding a layer of quiet sorrow. The disciples react with much calmer, subdued body language. ### Context Behind the Changes Art historians often link the stark shift in tone to the dramatic changes in Caravaggio’s personal life. By 1606, Caravaggio had fled Rome as an outlaw after killing a man in a brawl. Working under duress and in exile, his later style became much more psychological, raw, and focused on spiritual gravity rather than external performance.
Gemini told me from the first prompt that there are two versions of this painting. The one that I found was made in 1606 and another version was made earlier: Supper at Emmaus (1601).
